title: "Talking About Solo Travel"
description: "I know why I enjoy traveling alone, but describing it in English was harder than I expected."
pubDate: "Oct 2 2026"
---

Recently, I was chatting with a coworker about traveling, and I mentioned that I really enjoy traveling solo.

I've taken quite a few trips by myself, so I thought this would be an easy topic for me to talk about. But once I started explaining why I like solo travel, I realized that I didn't have all the words and expressions I wanted.

I knew what I meant. I just didn't know how to say it naturally in English.

One thing I like about traveling alone is that I can **go at my own pace**. I don't have to coordinate with anyone else or worry about whether someone is tired, hungry, or interested in the same things I am.

I can also **change my plans on a whim**. If I like a place, I can stay longer. If I don't, I can simply move on.

And sometimes I just enjoy **wandering around** without having a detailed plan. There is something nice about having nowhere I absolutely need to be.

Another thing I like about solo travel is that it makes it easier to meet new people. When I'm traveling with friends, I tend to stay in my own little group. But when I'm alone, I'm much more likely to talk to strangers, join a conversation, or meet someone along the way. I've met a lot of kind and interesting people that way.

Talking about solo travel made me realize that vocabulary isn't always about knowing the names of things. Sometimes it's about having the right expressions to describe experiences I've already had many times.

Here are a few expressions I want to remember:

- **travel solo / travel alone** — travel by yourself
- **go at my own pace** — do things as quickly or slowly as I want
- **change my plans on a whim** — suddenly change plans because I feel like it
- **wander around** — walk around without a particular destination
- **have the freedom to do something** — be able to make your own choices
- **play it by ear** — decide what to do as things happen

Apparently, even talking about something I know very well can turn into an English lesson.
