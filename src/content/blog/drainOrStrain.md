---
title: "Drain or Strain?"
description: "A small vocabulary lesson from cooking spaghetti."
pubDate: "Oct 4 2026"
---

Tonight, I was making spaghetti when I realized I wasn't sure how to describe one of the most basic cooking steps in English:

getting the water out of the pot.

My first thought was *drain*.

But then I wondered: do you *drain the pasta*, or do you *drain the pot*?

The most natural expression is:

*"Drain the pasta."*

It means to remove the water from the pasta, usually by pouring everything into a colander.

Then there's another word: strain.

You can also *strain the pasta*, especially if you're emphasizing the fact that you're using a strainer or colander to separate the pasta from the water.

So the difference is roughly:

- *drain the pasta* — focus on getting rid of the water
- *strain the pasta* — focus on separating the pasta from the water using a strainer

In everyday cooking, though, *"drain the pasta"* is probably what I'd say most often.
