---
title: "Office Hours, Workgroups, and Workshops"
description: "Just realize these three words don't mean the same thing."
pubDate: "Oct 5 2026"
---

I've been living in the Netherlands for over a year now, and I only recently realized that office hours, workgroups, and workshops are actually three different things.

Somehow, I had been treating them as roughly the same idea in my head.

When I was at CMU, I was very familiar with office hours. A professor or TA would set aside a few hours each week, and students could drop by to ask questions about lectures, homework, exams, or whatever they were stuck on.

At Leiden, though, I started seeing workgroup everywhere.

A workgroup is more like a small class where students actively work with the course material. Depending on the course, that might mean discussing readings, solving exercises, working on assignments, or giving presentations.

So, very roughly:

- *office hours*: "I have a question. Can you help me?"
- *workgroup*: "Let's actually work through the course material together."
- *workshop*: "Let's learn or practice a particular skill."

A workshop is usually more focused and hands-on. For example, you might attend a workshop on academic writing, coding, interviewing, or presentation skills.
