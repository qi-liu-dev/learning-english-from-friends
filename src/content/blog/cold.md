---
title: "When Cold Feels Like Needles"
description: "Trying to describe what −40°C actually feels like."
pubDate: "Oct 3 2026"
---

I was talking to a friend about a winter trip to Alaska and trying to describe what it felt like to walk outside at around −40°C, north of the Arctic Circle.

Even in the middle of the day, the cold was brutal.

I remember my feet hurting so badly that it felt like thousands of tiny needles were stabbing them with every step.

And then I got stuck on a surprisingly simple question:

How do you describe that kind of pain in English?

At first, the word that came to mind was *spike*, but that wasn't quite right. What I really meant was that my feet were *stinging from the cold*.

If I wanted to describe the pain more vividly, I could say:

"I felt a sharp, stabbing pain in my feet."

At −40°C, honestly, *stabbing* feels pretty accurate.

And one more funny detail: this happened in Wiseman, not far from a place literally called Coldfoot. So I guess I had cold feet near Coldfoot. Literally.
